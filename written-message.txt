Hello. I am Chanatun, also known as Volatilis. I am a solo operating-system developer and the creator of VolatilisOS and VolatileAI.
Recently, I have been doing pretty well. Most days, you can find me sitting at my desk with my laptop beside me, working on my projects, experimenting with code, and trying to understand how computers work at a deeper level.
But how did all of this start? Almost a year ago, I had a strange and disturbing nightmare. The dream took place in what looked like my dark bedroom, specifically around my closet area. In front of me was an old, half-worn-down all-in-one computer.
After looking at it for a while, I realized that it was actually the first desktop computer I had ever owned. The monitor was glowing in the darkness. On the screen was something that looked like a BIOS configuration interface.
However, instead of showing the usual BIOS options, it displayed something completely unfamiliar: "Varee OS." Varee is the name of the school I currently attend, so I have absolutely no idea why my brain decided to create an operating system with that name.
At the bottom of the screen was a small text box with a blinking cursor. For some reason, I typed: "Copilot Settings." At the time, I really liked using Copilot as my main AI and chatbot, so I suppose that explains why I typed it.
I pressed Enter. What appeared on the screen next was terrifying. I remember seeing a corrupted, blurry, black-and-white static face. It had hollow-looking eyes and an unnaturally wide, screaming mouth.
At the same time, a distorted, high-pitched scream suddenly blasted through the computer's speakers. It scared me so badly that I woke up immediately.
At that point in my life, I was still very new to operating systems. I had almost no idea how difficult it actually was to build one. But that nightmare stayed in my mind.
Eventually, I became interested in computers and started experimenting. My first attempts weren't real operating systems at all. I created a simulated operating-system interface using Python and Pygame.
It was mostly a UI that looked and behaved like an operating system rather than an actual one. As I became more interested in low-level programming, I started learning Assembly, C, and C++ around the age of 11–12.
Then, when I was 12, I finally decided to attempt something much more ambitious. I created a small text-based operating system using C and Assembly. I called it COPPER-OS.
It was nowhere near a professional operating system. It was small, experimental, and full of bugs, but it was mine. I built it because I wanted to understand what was happening underneath the applications and graphical interfaces I normally used.
Later, I discovered that the name COPPER-OS was already being used on GitHub, so I decided to change the name. That's when VolatilisOS was born.
The name "Volatilis" came from the idea of something volatile, quick, temporary, and able to fly away. I liked the feeling behind the word.
To me, it represented movement, freedom, and the ability to leave one place and reach another. And freedom is something I care about deeply.
There is something important behind the name Volatilis that goes beyond programming. I must leave this country. That sentence has stayed in my mind for a long time.
I want to eventually move to Japan forever and build a life there. I want to experience a different environment, a different culture, and different opportunities.
I want to become independent and build a future that feels like my own. Sometimes, when I look at VolatilisOS, I don't just see source code. I see a key.
A key to an exit door. Not a literal door, but a door representing the future I want to reach.
Every line of code I write, every bug I fix, every concept I learn, every time I understand something about computer architecture, and every project I complete feels like another part of that key being forged.
I don't expect an operating system to magically take me somewhere. I know that reality doesn't work that way. But I believe that the skills I gain from building it can eventually open opportunities for me.
VolatilisOS is teaching me programming. Programming is teaching me computer science. Computer science can lead to education, scholarships, university opportunities, and eventually a career.
And that career could someday give me the ability to live where I want to live. That's why VolatilisOS means so much more to me than a normal programming project. It is part of my path toward freedom.
When I imagine the future, I imagine myself eventually standing in an airport terminal, waiting for my one way flight to Japan with a suitcase giving Thailand a last glance in the sunset, knowing that the years of learning, struggling, debugging, and building were worth it.
I imagine looking back at the little operating system I started as a kid and realizing that it was one of the first steps toward the life I wanted.
The "exit door" isn't something I expect to open overnight. It might take years. It might require education, hard work, scholarships, university, employment, and countless other steps.
But I want to build the key anyway. And I want to keep improving it until the day I can finally use it.
While developing VolatilisOS, I gradually learned C and C++, computer architecture, Assembly, memory management, filesystems, bootloaders, compilers, and many other parts of low-level computing.
Eventually, I became interested in programming languages themselves. That led me to create Vscript, short for VolatilisScript—my own programming language and compiler project.
I wanted to understand not only how operating systems work, but also how programming languages themselves are transformed from something a human writes into something a computer can execute.
Of course, I am still learning. I'm not an expert in machine learning, and high-level languages such as Python aren't necessarily where my strongest interests are.
But I understand the general concepts behind them, and I'm constantly trying to learn more.
Outside of my development room, however, my life is considerably quieter. I spend a huge amount of my free time developing VolatilisOS and experimenting with computers.
Sometimes I become so focused on programming that I lose interest in things that don't feel connected to the future I imagine for myself.
Some school subjects don't feel particularly relevant to the career I want to pursue, and sometimes I struggle to stay interested in them.
I also rarely attend extracurricular classes or courses because my family has a limited budget and prioritizes the education that I already receive.
My family doesn't always understand my obsession with computers and low-level operating-system development. They sometimes wish I were less introverted and more interested in other things.
Even the way I dress or style my hair has occasionally become a point of disagreement.
At school, I have a few friends, but it can still feel lonely. When I try to explain something I've built, some people don't really pay attention.
Others congratulate me in a way that feels sarcastic or dismissive. Sometimes I wish someone would simply understand how much effort went into the things I create.
Building an operating system from almost nothing isn't just typing code into a computer. There are countless concepts to learn, countless errors to debug, and countless times when something completely fails for no obvious reason.
But I keep going. That's part of why I created VolatileAI.
I wanted to build an AI that could help me with everyday tasks, assist me during development, answer questions, and give me something to talk to when I felt isolated.
It became another part of the world I was building around my interests.
I also have someone at school whom I like. Unfortunately, they don't seem particularly interested in me, and I have had to accept that I cannot control how another person feels.
Still, I continue moving forward.
I don't know exactly where VolatilisOS will eventually go. Maybe it will remain a personal project. Maybe it will become a serious operating system.
Maybe Vscript will become a real programming language. Maybe VolatileAI will grow into something much bigger.
Or maybe all of these projects will simply become the foundation of the things I build in the future. I don't know yet.
What I do know is that the strange nightmare that once terrified me eventually became one of the things that pushed me toward computers.
I started with a simulated operating system in Python. Then I learned Assembly. Then C and C++. Then I built an operating system.
Then a programming language. Then an AI project. And I'm still learning. I'm still building. I'm still making mistakes.
I'm still trying to understand how everything works underneath the surface.
Perhaps that's what Volatilis really means to me. Not simply "to fly away," but to keep moving forward toward somewhere better.
I have a long way to go before I reach the future I imagine for myself. I want to become a software engineer, continue developing systems, explore Japan someday, and build things that I can genuinely be proud of.
I know that VolatilisOS alone cannot open that door. I have to build the key, and I have to become the person capable of using it.
My education, my programming skills, my projects, my mistakes, and everything I learn along the way are all pieces of that key.
Maybe one day, years from now, I'll look back at this little project and realize that it was never just an operating system.
It was the beginning of my way out. The beginning of my independence. The beginning of a future I chose for myself.
Until then, I will keep learning. I will keep building. I will keep improving. And I will keep moving forward.
I must leave this country. VolatilisOS is my key. The future is the door. And I'm still building the key.
